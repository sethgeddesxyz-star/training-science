# Warm-up and Mobility — the general warm-up, joint prep by day type, and the stretching policy (added 2026-10-25, user request)

Three files said "after the general warm-up" and none said what it was. The §10 warmup ramp
is a load ramp for the first compounds; prehab is per-injury activation; neither raises
body temperature or takes the day's joints through their range before loading. This file
defines the general warm-up (short, fixed, never cut), a one-round joint-prep block per day
type, what the engine does about stretching (little, deliberately), and how a range-of-motion
limit found in a lift is handled. Mobility snacks on rest days stay in `quick-sessions.md`
§3.9.

Read §1–§2 on every lifting session (they are what prints). §3–§4 only when the user asks
about stretching or flexibility, or a lift is limited by range.

---

## 1. General warm-up (every lifting session, 3 min in the estimate, never cut)

In order, before prehab and before the §10 ramp:

1. **Raise, 2 min**: easy cardio on whatever is there: bike, stairs, brisk march, jump rope
   easy, rower. Breathing up, not hard. On a `SORE` readiness flag this is 3 min.
2. **Joint prep, one round, about 1 min**: the day type's block from §2, no rest, no load
   beyond a light band. Any item already in the user's prehab block is not repeated: prehab
   covers it.

Then the prehab block (`injury-protocols.md`), then the §10 ramp, then working sets.

In the duration estimate (`output-format.md`) the general warm-up is a fixed **180 s**. It is
inside the time budget, and like prehab and the ramp it is never a cut candidate.

HIIT sessions use their own warm-up (`conditioning-hiit.md` §9). Kettlebell and strap
sessions use the warm-up rounds in `kettlebell.md` and `suspension-trainer.md` where one is
given; otherwise this one. Quick sessions and aerobic sessions do not take it.

## 2. Joint prep by day type

| Day type (`splits.md` §2) | Items (one round) |
|---|---|
| `PUSH`, `UPPER` | Shoulder CARs 3/side, slow · Scapular push-up 8 · Band pull-apart 10 · Open book 5/side |
| `PULL` | Dead hang 20 s · Scapular pull 8 · Band face pull 10 · Open book 5/side |
| `LEGS`, `LOWER` | Hip CARs 3/side · Knee-to-wall ankle rock 8/side · Glute bridge 10 · Bodyweight squat 8 with a 2-s pause at the bottom |
| `FB-A`, `FB-B` | Shoulder CARs 3/side · Hip CARs 3/side · Knee-to-wall ankle rock 8/side · Scapular push-up 8 · Bodyweight squat 8 with pause |

Rules:

1. An item that reproduces a pain is deleted for that user and noted in
   `injury-protocols.md`; it is not a flag by itself.
2. The block is fixed per day type, like prehab: never assembled fresh, never extended into
   a mobility session. One round.
3. A knee flag swaps the paused squat for a wall sit 20 s; a shoulder flag drops shoulder
   CARs to pain-free range and keeps them.

---

## 3. Stretching policy

1. **No static stretching before working sets** beyond 30 s per muscle. Longer holds before
   lifting cost a little force and nothing in injury terms is gained (§5). A short hold is
   allowed when a range limit is blocking the lift today (ankle before squats, hip flexors
   before hinges): ≤ 30 s, once, then the ramp.
2. **Lifting through a full range is the flexibility program.** Every lift in the library is
   prescribed at full pain-free range. For most users that is all the stretching they need,
   and it is measured by the lifts themselves.
3. **A flexibility goal is optional and user-declared.** If the user wants more range in a
   named joint (ranked goal, or a lift limited by range per §4), the dose is **60 s per muscle
   per bout, 2–3 bouts a week, after a session or on a rest day**, logged as a mobility
   quick session (`quick-sessions.md` §3.9). Static or dynamic, the user's choice. No
   stretching for its own sake is ever added to a sheet.
4. **Mobility on deload weeks** is the §3.9 snack, any day, as `quick-sessions.md` §2 already
   allows. Nothing else changes.

## 4. A range limit found in a lift

"Can't get to depth", "shoulders won't let me press overhead", "hamstrings stop the RDL" is
not pain and not a flag. It is handled in this order:

1. **Regress the variant, keep the pattern**: the library's limited-range or supported
   option (box or limited-ROM squat, floor press, elevated RDL, landmine-style press with
   DBs, TRX-assisted squat). Progress by range before load: the same load at a deeper box,
   then the normal decision tree.
2. **Add the limiting joint's item to §2 for that user**, daily if they want, as a mobility
   snack: ankle rocks, hip CARs, 90/90, thoracic extension over a bench. Logged like any
   snack.
3. **Re-test at the block boundary**: the full-range variant at the Unknown exercise gate
   (conservative load, calibration). If it is clean, the limited variant is retired; if not,
   another block on step 1 and 2.
4. A range limit with a sensation attached is a pain report and goes to
   `return-to-training.md`.

## 5. Why (evidence, brief)

- **Warming up improves performance; it is the one part of pre-exercise routine with
  consistent evidence.** A meta-analysis of 32 studies found warm-up improved performance in
  79% of criteria examined (Fradkin 2010). The mechanisms are temperature, blood flow and
  post-activation potentiation, and the effective form is short, specific and progressive:
  raise, mobilise, potentiate, which is the three-part structure here (McGowan 2015, the
  RAMP framework). Three minutes is the low end of what the reviews used; the §10 ramp
  supplies the potentiation.
- **Static stretching before lifting costs a little and prevents nothing.** Holds over
  about 60 s reduce force and power acutely by a few percent; holds under 30 s have a
  trivial effect; and pre-exercise static stretching does not reduce injury in the
  all-cause sense (Behm 2016, systematic review). Strength training reduces sports injury
  by about two thirds in randomised trials; stretching programmes show no effect
  (Lauersen 2014). Hence rule 1 of §3, and hence prehab (loaded activation) instead of
  stretching as the injury layer.
- **Lifting through a full range improves flexibility as much as stretching.** Strength
  training and stretching produced equivalent gains in range of motion across 11 trials
  (Afonso 2021). That is why the default flexibility program is the program.
- **Flexibility as a fitness component is over-rated**, with weak links to health outcomes
  and performance outside specific sports (Nuzzo 2020). The engine therefore treats range
  as a goal only when the user names one or a lift is limited by it, not as a default
  tracked quantity.
- **When range is the goal, the dose is modest.** About 5 min of stretching per muscle per
  week, spread over sessions, improves range; more per session adds little (Thomas 2018).
  §3 rule 3 is that dose.
