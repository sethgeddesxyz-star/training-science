# Conditioning — HIIT / VO2max Slot (added 2026-09-25, user request)

A fourth day type in the rotation: a short, hard interval session aimed at VO2max, built from
the protocols with the strongest evidence and rotated across formats so the stimulus stays
varied. It is **part of the rotation, not an optional extra** — it comes due like any other day
type and stays due until it is done. Per-user state (format cycle position, modality, loads,
HRmax, log) lives in `users/<name>/training-state.md` under **Conditioning — HIIT**.

---

## 1. The science, briefly (references in §10)

- **Why VO2max.** Cardiorespiratory fitness is one of the strongest predictors of all-cause
  mortality: elite vs low fitness HR 5.04 across 122,007 treadmill tests with no upper limit
  (Mandsager 2018); each +1 MET (3.5 mL/kg/min) ≈ 13% lower all-cause mortality (Kodama 2009).
  The user's #1 ranked goal is longevity.
- **Intensity drives it.** In moderately trained men, 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax raised VO2max
  +7.2% in 8 weeks, and 15/15 s intervals +5.5%; work-matched steady and threshold running did
  nothing significant (Helgerud 2007). Meta-analyses agree: HIIT beats moderate continuous work
  for VO2max (Milanović 2015; Poon 2024 umbrella review). The largest gains come from **reps of
  ≥ 2 min and ≥ 15 min of hard work per session**, over 4–12 weeks (Wen 2019).
- **The key variable is time spent near VO2max** — aim for about ≥ 10 min above ~90% VO2max per
  session (Buchheit & Laursen 2013).
- **Short intervals work too, differently.** 30/15 s (Rønnestad 2015, 2020) and 10-20-30
  (Gunnarsson & Bangsbo 2012) hold VO2 high with less perceived strain. Sprint-interval
  training (4–6 × 30 s all-out, or just 2–3 × 20 s all-out in a 10-min session) raises VO2max
  about 7–8% (Gist 2014), +19% in sedentary men (Gillen 2016), with gains mostly **peripheral**
  (muscle mitochondria). Long intervals add **central** gains (stroke volume, cardiac output)
  (Rosenblat 2022). Doing both covers both limits — that is the case for rotating formats.
- **"Myokinase."** The myokinase (adenylate kinase) reaction, 2 ADP → ATP + AMP, is the muscle's
  energy-stress sensor: a small drop in ATP gives a large rise in AMP, which activates AMPK and
  PGC-1α (mitochondrial biogenesis) (Dzeja & Terzic 2009; Hardie 2012; Gibala 2009). All-out
  sprints produce the sharpest AMP/ATP stress, which is what fitness media mean by "myokinase
  training". It is **not a separate energy system or a recognised training category**. Energy
  systems overlap (Gastin 2001), and every hard interval format triggers this signalling, with
  intensity mattering more than pathway (MacInnis & Gibala 2017). In this program, Format C
  (sprints) is the "myokinase" session.
- **Lifting interference is small at this dose.** Concurrent training does not blunt muscle
  growth or max strength overall (Schumann 2022, 43 studies). HIIT specifically: no hypertrophy
  or upper-body cost, a small lower-body strength cost (ES −0.25) that **disappears with ≥ 24 h
  separation** (Sabag 2018). The cost grows with frequency and duration, so keep to ≤ 3 cardio
  sessions/week (Wilson 2012). Same day: lift first (Murlasits 2018); ≥ 6 h apart is better than
  0 h (Robineau 2016).
- **Dose.** 2 sessions/week is enough to build (Generation 100; REHIT frequency data); 3/week
  gave the largest RCT gains but costs the most recovery. **Maintenance: VO2max holds on 2
  sessions/week, or even much less duration, as long as intensity is kept** (Hickson 1981,
  1982); cutting intensity loses it (Hickson 1985). Gains show up in 2–4 weeks, and most arrive
  by 6–12 weeks. Expect about +5–10%. "Non-responders" usually respond to more dose (Montero &
  Lundby 2017).
- **Fat loss.** HIIT and moderate cardio lose similar fat; HIIT takes about 40% less time
  (Wewege 2017). Diet drives fat loss. (Viana 2019, often cited here, was **retracted** — do
  not cite it.)

## 2. Rotation placement

- **Rotation: PUSH → LEGS → PULL → HIIT.** HIIT sits after pull and before push, which puts it
  as far from leg day as a 4-slot cycle allows (two sessions after legs, two before the next).
- **HIIT is due** when it is the next day type in the rotation. It stays due until performed.
- **Opener handling:**
  - "Gym day" / "workout today" with no day type, and HIIT is next → prescribe HIIT.
  - The user names a lifting day while HIIT is due → prescribe that day as asked, keep HIIT
    due, and add one line to the Session Notes: "HIIT due — [N] days since last."
  - "HIIT", "intervals", "cardio day" → prescribe HIIT whatever is next. If this jumps the
    rotation, the rotation resumes from the day after the performed HIIT slot (as if it came
    up in order); the skipped lifting day type stays next.
- **Stacking.** HIIT may be stacked **after a pull session** (lift first, HIIT after) on the same
  visit. That counts as the HIIT slot, and the next day type becomes PUSH. Never stack it after
  legs or before any lifting session.
- **Bonus second HIIT (to reach 2/week, optional).** Allowed on a non-lifting day when (a) the
  next day's session is not legs, and (b) it is at least 24 h after the last legs session. Run
  the next format in the cycle; it advances the cycle but not the rotation.
- **Weekly cap:** 3 HIIT sessions per 7 days, bonus included. Incline-walk zone-2 work does not
  count toward the cap.

## 3. Formats — the diversified cycle

HIIT sessions cycle through **A → B → A → C** and then repeat. Long intervals (A) anchor the
cycle because they carry both central and peripheral gains; B and C add variety and the
peripheral / sprint stimulus. The cycle position persists across weeks and blocks.

Every session: **warm-up 8–10 min easy, building to moderate, plus 2–3 pickups of 20 s**; then
the main set; **cool-down 3–5 min easy** (never stop dead after the last rep).

### Format A — Norwegian 4×4 (long intervals; VO2max anchor)
- **4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax (RPE 16–18 on the 6–20 scale — only a few words possible),
  3 min active recovery at ~70% HRmax.** About 35 min total with the warm-up.
- HR should reach 90% HRmax by minute 2–3 of each rep; if it doesn't, raise the output for the
  next rep.
- **Entry: 3 × 4 min** for the first two A sessions, then 4 × 4.

### Format B — Short intervals, 30/15 (Rønnestad)
- **3 sets × 13 × (30 s hard / 15 s easy spin), 3 min easy between sets.** ~20 min of work
  blocks. Hard = the highest output that can be **held evenly across all 13 reps** (RPE 17–18);
  HR lags and only reaches ~90% late in each set, so pace by output and RPE, not HR.
- **Entry: 2 sets × 13** for the first B session.
- Home variant: **Two-hand KB swing 15 s on / 15 s off × 20–30 rounds** (Falatic 2015 used
  snatches; two-hand swings keep the shoulder quiet). Bell: 35 lb or 53 lb, whichever keeps
  crisp hip snap on every rep. Stop a round when form breaks.

### Format C — Sprint intervals (the "myokinase" day)
- **Bike only** (all-out running sprints carry hamstring-strain risk and flat-ground knee
  load; see the injury file). Air bike acceptable if the shoulder is quiet.
- **4 × 20 s all-out against heavy resistance, 3 min very easy spin between.** About 20 min
  total with the warm-up. All-out means all-out; this is the one format that must feel maximal
  from the first pedal stroke.
- **Entry: 2 × 20 s** (REHIT, Metcalfe 2012) for the first C session, then 3, then 4. Evidence
  shows extra sprints beyond 2–3 add little for VO2max (Vollaard 2017), so the cap is 4.
- Home variant: **8 × 10 s hill or stair sprint, walk down + 2 min rest** (Hazell 2010), or
  skip C at home and run A or B instead (the cycle advances the same).

### Deload weeks
When the lifting block is in its deload week, HIIT runs **once, at the same intensity, half the
reps** (4×4 → 2×4, 3 sets → 1–2 sets, sprints 4 → 2). Keep intensity and cut volume, per
Hickson. HIIT is **not** a lifting day type for deload rotation coverage (§6 of the progression
rules). The cycle position still advances.

## 4. Modality

Choose by the joints, not by interference; the running-vs-cycling interference data conflict.

| Modality | Formats | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bike (upright/spin)** | A, B, C | **Default.** Almost no eccentric loading, easiest on the knee. Saddle high enough for ~25–35° knee bend at the bottom; favour cadence (≥ 85–90 rpm) over grinding heavy resistance, except the C sprints. |
| **Rower** | A, B | Good second option. Deep knee bend at the catch — shorten the catch if the knee comments. Loads the lats and lower back, but the HIIT slot sits after pull, so pull fatigue can overlap: if the back or lats are still sore from pull, use the bike. |
| **Stair climber** | A | Knee-friendly at steady cadence; good for 4×4 variety. |
| **Incline treadmill (hill walk/jog, ≥ 10%)** | A | Uphill cuts impact and eccentric load. Power-walk or jog at the incline needed to hit 90% HRmax. |
| **Air bike** | A, B, C | If available; arms add shoulder work — shoulder gate applies. |
| **Home: KB swing, hill/stairs** | B (swing), C (hill/stair sprints) | See the format variants above. |

**Diversify:** rotate modality by format — **A alternates bike → rower → stair/treadmill** across
successive A sessions; B alternates bike ↔ rower; C is always bike. This gives novel stimulus
without new skills. Log the modality on every session, because output numbers are
modality-specific.

## 5. Intensity, HRmax and progression

- **HRmax**: use the highest HR actually observed in a hard session (an A rep 4 or the end of a
  B set). Until one exists, estimate with **Tanaka: 208 − 0.7 × age** (±10 bpm). No HR monitor →
  RPE only (A: 16–18; B: 17–18; C: maximal).
- **Output is the load.** Log average watts (bike/rower/air bike), pace (/500 m rower) or
  speed + incline (treadmill) per rep or set. Progress **one variable at a time, like double
  progression**:
  1. **Reps / sets** up to the format's full dose (entry doses above).
  2. **Output**: once the full dose is completed with the last rep/set at or above the first
     one's output (no fade), raise the target output next time of that format + modality by
     one step (bike +5–10 W; rower −1 s/500 m; treadmill +0.2 mph or +1% incline).
  3. If the target output fades by more than ~5% by the last rep, hold it.
- **Failure / bad day**: output drops ≥ 10% on the first reps, or HR won't climb → finish at
  RPE, hold targets, and treat it as a fatigue signal (§6).

## 6. Fatigue governance — HIIT gets cut first

Cut or swap the HIIT session for 20 min of easy zone 2 (or skip it), **before** touching any
lifting, when any of these hold:

- A §8 reactive-deload trigger has fired, or ≥ 2 main lifts regressed across 2 sessions.
- The user reports resting HR up ≥ 5 bpm for several days, or HRV down for several days (only
  if they track it).
- Poor sleep, illness, or a knee/shoulder Pain Response Protocol opened in the last 48 h (the
  HIIT still runs if the modality avoids the joint entirely, e.g. bike with a shoulder event).
- The next day is legs (this should not happen with correct placement, but a bonus session can
  cause it).

An engine-driven swap does not advance the format cycle; the same format is prescribed next
time.

## 7. Benchmark (every 8 weeks)

Track VO2max by output, not by a lab test. Run a benchmark as an A-slot session every 8
weeks (and once before the first A session if the user wants a baseline):
- **Bike 20-min steady test at a fixed wattage** (the same number every time) → record the
  average HR over the last 10 min. Lower HR at the same watts = aerobic gain.
- **Plus a 5-min max effort** (bike or rower, same machine every time) → average watts. That is
  a direct proxy for maximal aerobic power.
- Wearable VO2max estimates are fine as a trend line only.

## 8. Safety

- Screening (PAR-Q+/ACSM): someone without symptoms or known cardiac, metabolic or renal
  disease may build up to vigorous exercise gradually without clearance.
- **Stop and see a doctor** for chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting, unusual
  breathlessness, or palpitations. The trainer asks about these once before the first HIIT
  session and records the answer in the state.
- The first 2 weeks run at the entry doses, finishing each at RPE ≤ 17. Build before going
  maximal.
- No heavy pre-workout or stimulant use before sprints.

## 9. Output and reporting

**Coaching view** for a HIIT day uses the standard header (`## [Day] — [Location] (HIIT —
Format [A/B/C])`, Block and Week from the lifting block) and replaces Working Sets with:

```
### Warm-up
- [Modality]: 8–10 min easy → moderate, 3 × 20 s pickups

### Main Set — Format [X]: [name]
1. **[reps] × [work] @ [target output] ([HR/RPE target])** | Recovery: [time @ easy output]
   > Progression note: [why this output/rep count]
   > Last [format] session: [what was done]

### Cool-down
- [Modality]: 3–5 min easy

### Session Notes
- Estimated duration: ~[X] min · HIIT this week: [n]/2 · Cycle: [A→B→A→C, position]
```

**Checklist**, same rules as lifting (one line per rep or set, self-contained, rest line above):

```
2026-09-27 — Gym (HIIT — Format A) — Hypertrophy Wk 2/5

WARMUP
Bike — 10 min — easy → moderate + 3 × 20 s pickups

WORKING
4x4 Bike — R3m @ 70% HRmax easy spin
4x4 Bike — Rep 1 — 4 min — [target W] — HR 90–95%
4x4 Bike — Rep 2 — 4 min — [target W] — HR 90–95%

COOLDOWN
Bike — 5 min — easy
```

**Reporting:** unchanged line = completed at the target output. A changed number = the actual
output. Appended HR = peak HR for that rep (update HRmax if it is a new high). "Fade", "died",
"cut short" = the §5 step-3 hold. Skipped session = HIIT stays due; not a miss in any
progression sense.
