# Suspension Trainer (TRX) Repertoire (added 2026-10-18, user request)

A suspension trainer (TRX-style straps; gymnastic rings count too) is (a) the most complete
"anywhere" kit the program has: one door anchor, tree or beam covers every pattern except a heavy
hinge; (b) a modality family, tagged `TRX`, for the Accessory Rotation (`sbs-progression-rules.md`
§6) and the modality-rotation rule in `system/modality-rotation.md`; (c) the natural home/travel
substitute for the Tier-1 row and press slots when no dumbbells are to hand; (d) a source of core
co-contraction and single-leg work that machines do not give; and (e) the building blocks of the
short sessions in `system/quick-sessions.md`. It is **never a main-lift tier for push or pull**
unless the user has no other load at all. Strap exercises run in **ladder mode**
(`bodyweight-ladders.md` general rules) where the step is a (mark, variation) pair (§2). Per-user
state lives in `users/<name>/training-state.md` under **Suspension Trainer** (§7). Injury flags
here are generic and always defer to the user's private `users/<name>/injury-protocols.md`.

## 1. The science, briefly (references in §10)

- **The straps add instability and core demand, not load.** A suspended push-up gives higher pec,
  front-delt and triceps EMG than a floor push-up (Snarr & Esco 2013a) and rectus abdominis
  activity of ~68% MVIC against ~21% on the floor, on a par with a crunch (Snarr & Esco 2013c).
  Across four suspension systems the abs are highly active on every device, while the pecs and
  front delts are better served by stable set-ups (Calatayud 2014). **A strap press is a
  push-plus-plank**, not a heavier push.
- **Rows are the exception.** The suspended inverted row matches the fixed-bar row for the
  posterior chain and is slightly lower for the biceps (Snarr & Esco 2013b); the 18-study
  systematic review finds suspension versions beat their traditional twins for upper-body and core
  activation **except the inverted row** (Aguilera-Castells 2020). The TRX row is a fair row.
- **Core work on straps is genuinely hard.** Suspended planks raise rectus abdominis demand (Byrne
  2014); of four classic TRX moves, hip abduction in plank gives the highest abdominal activity and
  the hamstring curl the highest lumbar multifidus activity (Mok 2015; Fong 2015 in chronic
  low-back-pain patients); rollout and body saw drive rectus abdominis and external oblique to
  ~55–60% MVIC (Cugliari & Boccia 2017); the suspended pike out-activates the BOSU and Swiss-ball
  pike for the obliques (Snarr 2016). Suspended pushes carry more spine compression than stable
  ones, and technique coaching changes the numbers (McGill 2014): the back-flag rules in §3 follow.
- **Load is a function of angle and strap length.** Strap tension in a push-up rises as the straps
  approach vertical: ~50% BW on the straps with elbows extended, ~75% with elbows flexed at the
  steepest setting (Gulmez 2017); longer straps lower body inclination and ground reaction force
  and raise strap load, and the authors propose strap length as the progression variable
  (Giancotti 2018); resistance by angle and distance from the anchor was mapped by Melrose & Dawes
  (2015). This is the basis of the §2 mark scale.
- **Training trials: parity with conventional training in the people studied, not superiority.**
  Seven weeks of BOSU/TRX circuits matched a stable circuit for 1RM, power and jump in untrained
  men (Maté-Muñoz 2014); TRX and traditional programs gave similar strength, endurance, flexibility
  and balance gains in younger and middle-aged adults (Janot 2013); a 12-week TRX program in older
  adults was feasible at 85% compliance (Gaedtke & Morat 2015), and 12 weeks of suspension training
  beat band/bodyweight work for grip strength and muscle mass in older men (Campa 2021). A 60-min
  interval TRX workout averaged ~69% HRmax, lactate ~8 mmol/L: moderate conditioning (Dudgeon 2015).
- **The limit.** Instability cuts force, power and velocity, which is why the CSEP position stand
  puts ground-based free-weight lifts at the foundation (Behm 2010). **Most evidence here is acute
  EMG, not training trials**; the trials are in untrained or older people and show parity; **nothing
  shows straps beat loaded training for hypertrophy or max strength.** Their defensible value is
  core co-contraction, finely scalable unilateral work, joint-friendly pulling, and portability.

## 2. How load works on straps: the angle rule

Resistance is a fraction of bodyweight set by **body angle to the floor**, which is set by **where
the feet stand relative to the anchor line** (the floor point under the anchor). For rows and
presses alike: **feet closer to, or past, the anchor line = steeper lean = heavier.** Elbow flexion
adds load on top (Gulmez 2017), so full range is part of the step and a short rep is a missed rep.

### Foot-position marks 0–8

| Mark | Body angle (approx.) | Feet | Load, % BW (approx.) |
|------|----------------------|------|----------------------|
| 0 | ~70° (near vertical) | far from the anchor line | ~20% |
| 1 | ~65° | one half-step closer per mark | ~27% |
| 2 | ~60° | | ~33% |
| 3 | ~52° | | ~40% |
| 4 | ~45° | | ~47% |
| 5 | ~38° | | ~54% |
| 6 | ~30° | | ~60% |
| 7 | ~20° | at the anchor line | ~67% |
| 8 | ~horizontal | past the anchor line, or feet on a box | ~75% |

Percentages are for ordering only, never an e1RM input (§6). One mark ≈ +5–8% BW.

**Calibration (once per anchor, per strap length, per family: row, press).** Set the strap length,
find the foot position giving ~70° and tape it as Mark 0, find ~horizontal and tape it as Mark 8,
tape 7 marks at equal spacing between. Record `marks calibrated: y, <date>` (§7). A new anchor or
a strap-length change for that family means recalibrating before the next prescription. Without
tape a mark is not reproducible and the ladders below are guesswork.

### Strap length settings

| Setting | Used for |
|---------|----------|
| Fully lengthened | Chest press, chest fly, tricep extension, assisted squat / pistol, Y/T/W, overhead reach |
| Mid-length | Row (all grips), face pull, high row, bicep curl, fallout, Pallof press, thoracic rotation |
| Mid-calf (handles at mid-calf) | Hamstring curl, hip press, any feet-in-cradles move, atomic push-up, suspended Bulgarian |
| Over-shortened (handles at chest standing under the anchor) | Single-arm row, single-arm press, archer row, rear-delt fly |

### Ladder mode on straps (general rules of `bodyweight-ladders.md` apply unchanged)

- **Step = (mark, variation).** Within a variation each mark is one step. **Advance** at 3 × 8 clean
  (3 × 30 s for holds) → next mark at 3 × 5. **Marks first, variations after:** Mark 8 at 3 × 8 →
  next variation in the §4 ladder, at the mark that row names, never Mark 8 of the new variation.
- **Entry gate, stall limit (3 sessions → gap bridge), pain rule, deload (65% volume)** as written
  there. Strap-friendly gap bridges: 3-s eccentric at the harder mark; hybrid set (first 5 reps at
  the harder mark, remainder at the current one); partial ROM at the harder mark.
- **Missed time** (`sbs-progression-rules.md` §9 ladder mode): **regress 1 mark per 14 full days
  away**, floor Mark 0, restart at 3 × 5. Layoff never drops a variation; only the entry gate does.
- A mark advance resets `stallCounter`; a session with no advance at effort "hard"/"failed"
  increments it.

Steps 1–9 of a marked ladder are Marks 0–8 of the base variation (step = mark + 1). Variations
begin at the mark the row names. Unilateral steps use `/side`.

## 5. Session templates

Durations are computed per `output-format.md`: (sets × reps × tempo s) + (sets − 1) × rest + 60 s
setup per exercise, then +15% margin. Tempo 3 s/rep (4 s for single-leg squats). Marks and steps
come from the user's state; templates fix slots, sets, reps and rest only. The general warm-up
(3–5 min easy movement, plus the I-Y-T set if a shoulder flag is active) is a WARMUP block inside
the quoted total. Sets count toward weekly volume at the usual 1.0× / 0.5× rates.

### (d) 10-min TRX circuit (detail in `system/quick-sessions.md`)

Six moves, **30 s work / 20 s change, 2 rounds = 10 min**: TRX Row (Mark 3) → TRX Chest Press
(Mark 3) → TRX Assisted Squat → TRX Hamstring Curl → TRX Fallout (kneeling if unsure) → TRX
Mountain Climber. Logged as a quick session; ladder state does not change.

### (e) Travel-week rule

Away **≥ 5 days with straps only**: run **TRX Full Body (a)** on the normal rotation cadence in
place of each lifting day type that comes due (HIIT runs its home variants). Ladder entries
progress normally. **The block week does not advance** in travel mode, mirroring how home-only
sessions substitute the Tier-1 member without advancing the tier; on return the block resumes at
the week it left. Set `travel mode: y` at the first travel session, clear it at the first session
back, and run the §9 missed-time check on the loaded lifts that were not trained.

**Cap on the hold.** Block weeks are counted per day type, so holding the week is consistent with
the deload-completion rule, but the §8 hard cap (never more than 6 loading weeks without a deload)
is a calendar rule. Travel mode may hold the week for **at most 14 days**; a trip longer than that
ends the block where it stands and the first week back is the deload (at 65% of the pre-trip
loads, after the §9 missed-time table has been applied), then the next block opens.

## 6. Where it slots in the rotation

- **Accessory pool members** tagged `TRX` are eligible for any accessory slot at any location whose
  `equipment-profiles.md` entry lists an anchor.
- **Ceiling ladder Rung 3** (`sbs-progression-rules.md` §5): a DB/machine exercise at its ceiling
  may swap to the unilateral strap version (single-arm row or press, single-leg curl, assisted
  pistol). The slot enters ladder mode at the mark the §4 ladder names and leaves it only by the
  user's choice at a block boundary.
- **Accessory Rotation replacement candidate** (§6 "choosing the replacement"): a `TRX` member meets
  rule 1 against a DB, cable or machine incumbent on two counts, position bias (bodyweight lever,
  hardest at the stretched bottom) and implement change. Rules 2–6 still apply. A `TRX` exercise
  rotated in as an accessory carries the accessory cadence (1-block hold, due at 2 boundaries) even
  though it is a ladder; its ladder state persists in §7 so it resumes where it left.
- **Deload-week variety session:** allowed, at the ladder deload (65% volume, same mark).
- **Standalone travel sessions:** §5(e).
- **What it does NOT do:** no main-lift tier progress (a strap row or press in a Tier-1 slot is a
  substitution, not a tier); no e1RM from a mark; no Strength-wave entry (in a Strength block a TRX
  exercise runs its ladder as written); no AMRAP sets.
- **Volume counting.** Ladder mode has no RIR target, so a low-mark set may be far from failure.
  A strap set counts toward the §11 weekly volume at the usual 1.0× / 0.5× rates when it is at
  **Mark 3 or higher**, or at any mark when the user reports it hard or it ends within 2 reps of
  the set's limit; otherwise it counts at half those rates. Circuit-style strap work in a quick
  session follows `quick-sessions.md` §2 instead.

A line is created at the first prescription and never deleted: a rotated-out exercise keeps its
line so it resumes at its step. Its Accessory Rotation line (name, block entered, keep used) lives
in the rotation table like any accessory.

## 9. Safety and setup

- **Anchor test before the first rep of every session:** hang full bodyweight from the handles,
  feet off the floor, 3 s. Anything that creaks, shifts or moves the door = no straps today.
- **Door anchor on the pull side only:** the door closes toward the user with the anchor on the far
  side, so load pulls the door into its frame; lock it. Never a door opening toward the user, a
  hollow door, or a slider.
- **Strap length check** at every exercise change: handles level, locking loops seated, no twist.
  Unequal straps are the commonest cause of a shoulder complaint on presses.
- **Never let a strap go slack.** Tension from first rep to last; a rep that goes slack ends the
  set. This is the rule that keeps fallouts, presses and mountain climbers safe.
- **Foot cradles:** heels or laces in, straps outside the ankles, feet set while sitting.
- **Abrasion:** hands slightly wider than the straps on rows and presses; inspect for fraying
  monthly and at every new anchor; retire a frayed strap.
- **First exposure to any new variation** is at the start target, never the advance target.
