# Power and Plyometrics Module — OPTIONAL (added 2026-10-26, user request)

An optional add-on that trains **how fast force is produced**: jumps, throws and an
explosive-intent cue on the main lifts. The SBS engine builds strength and muscle; nothing in
it asks for speed, and until this file the only jumping in the system lived inside the bone
module, so a user who declined bone loading never jumped. Power is the quality that falls
away fastest with age and the one most tied to everyday function (stairs, catching a
stumble, getting up fast), and it responds to small, frequent, high-quality doses.

**Optional means:** offered once at intake, never required, and skipping it is never a miss
and never costs progression anywhere else. **Encouraged means:** a rolling weekly tally with
a one-line nudge below minimum (§6), the same mechanism as `bone-loading.md` §5.

Read this file whenever a session is prescribed **while the module is active** (a rung or
tally recorded under **Power Module** in the user's `training-state.md`), when the opener
says "power", "jumps", "plyos" or "throws", and once at intake to offer it. While the state
says "Not started" the file is not read and no POWER section prints. Injury gates live in
`users/<name>/injury-protocols.md` and **always win** over this file.

## 1. What the science says (short version; references in §10)

- **Power is lost faster than strength.** Leg power declines at roughly 3–4% a year after
  65 against 1–2% for strength (Skelton 1994), and in older adults power predicts walking
  speed, stair climb and chair rise better than strength does (Bean 2002; Reid & Fielding
  2012). Frequent fallers have lower and more asymmetric leg power than non-fallers of the
  same strength (Skelton 2002).
- **Training for power improves function more than training for strength alone.** In
  older adults, explosive-intent resistance training beat conventional training on
  functional outcomes with the same safety record (Tschopp 2011 meta-analysis; Straight 2016
  meta-analysis). Lower-limb plyometric training in healthy older adults was safe in every
  trial reviewed and improved jump, strength and function (Vetrovsky 2019).
- **Plyometrics work at every age and need little volume.** Across 26 studies, jump
  training raised vertical jump about 8% (Markovic 2007) and improved maximal strength on
  its own (Sáez de Villarreal 2010). Effective programmes used 2 sessions a week and modest
  contact counts; more was not better (Ramírez-Campillo 2018 scoping review).
- **Intent is the stimulus.** Lifting a load with the intent to move it as fast as possible
  produces the velocity-specific adaptation whether or not the bar actually moves fast
  (Behm & Sale 1993). Maximal-intent concentrics gained about twice as much strength as
  deliberately slow ones at matched load and volume (González-Badillo 2014). That is why §3
  is a cue on the existing lifts, free of cost.
- **A few explosive reps before a heavy lift help rather than hurt**, and a heavy set
  before a jump potentiates the jump: contrast and complex pairing improves both when the
  explosive work is low-volume and fully rested (Seitz & Haff 2016; Cormier 2020). The §2
  primer uses the first half of that pairing.
- **Jump height is the most fatigue-sensitive number in training.** Countermovement jump
  output drops with accumulated fatigue and poor sleep before strength does (Claudino 2017
  meta-analysis). Power work on a flagged day is wasted at best; §5 skips it.
- **Dose guidance** (NSCA, Potach & Chu 2016; Davies 2015): beginners 80–100 ground
  contacts per session, 48–72 h between lower-body sessions, full recovery between sets,
  every rep at maximal intent, and quality cut-offs rather than rep targets. This module
  runs under those numbers.

## 2. Component A — the power primer (inside the session)

Three low-rep explosive sets after the general warm-up and prehab, **before the warmup
ramp** of the first main lift. It is the cheapest way to get power work into a week that is
already full, and it wakes the nervous system up for the lift that follows.

| Day's first main pattern | Primer movement | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| squat or hinge (`LEGS`, `LOWER`, `FB-A`, `FB-B`) | the **lower ladder** rung in state (§4) | 3 × 3–5, rest 60 s |
| h_push or v_push (`PUSH`, `UPPER`) | the **upper ladder** rung in state (§4) | 3 × 3–5, rest 60 s |
| h_pull or v_pull (`PULL`) | upper ladder rung, **or** no primer when the user prefers (pull days have no natural throw) | 3 × 3–5, rest 60 s |

Rules:

1. **Every rep is a maximal effort with a full reset.** Reps are not continuous; the user
   lands or catches, resets stance, then goes again. A set ends early when height, distance
   or snap visibly drops, and the set still counts.
2. **Fixed 180 s in the duration estimate** (`output-format.md`), inside the time budget.
   On a time-limited sheet the primer is cut **after Bone Doubles and before any
   accessory**: it is optional, and it must never push a programmed lift off the sheet.
3. Full body: one primer per session, on the day's leg pattern (squat on `FB-A`, hinge on
   `FB-B`), so the lower ladder gets both sessions and the upper ladder is served by
   standalone bouts (§4) or by the day's second primer only if the user asks for one.
4. **No primer** on a deload-parameter session under `ILL`, on any readiness flag (§5), in
   the session that opens a Pain Response Protocol, or when the ladder rung is paused by a
   gate (§4). A 2 × 3 primer runs in deload weeks (§5).
5. The primer does not count toward §11 volume, opens no e1RM thread and changes nothing
   in the lift that follows.

## 3. Component B — explosive intent on the lifts (a cue, not a prescription)

In **Strength blocks** every main-lift working rep carries the cue **"lower under control,
drive as fast as you can"**. The coaching view says it once on the first main lift
(`> Intent: fast concentric, every rep`) and the checklist is unchanged. In Hypertrophy
blocks the cue is not printed; the user may still lift that way.

Nothing else changes: load, reps, rest, RIR and the decision tree are exactly as
`sbs-progression-rules.md` writes them. The cue is free, the evidence is strong (§1), and it
is the one piece of this module that is on every Strength sheet while the module is active.

## 4. Component C — the ladders and standalone bouts

Ladders in the `bodyweight-ladders.md` sense: ordered rungs, each unlocked by clean bouts on
the one below. **Clean** = every rep at full intent, landings quiet and stuck (no stumble,
no knee cave), and no knee, Achilles, shin, foot, shoulder or back sensation during the bout
or the next morning.

### Lower ladder (jump)

| Rung | Movement | Dose per bout | Contacts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pogo hops, in place** — ankle-driven, knees nearly straight, quiet, fast off the floor | 3 × 10, rest 45 s | 30 | tissue calibration; matches `bone-loading.md` Rung 2 |
| 2 | **Box jump, step down** — jump onto a 12" box or step, land soft in a quarter squat, step down, reset | 4 × 4, rest 60 s | 16 | low landing force; the entry rung for anyone with a knee history |
| 3 | **Countermovement jump, stick** — quarter-squat dip, max vertical jump, land in a quarter squat and hold 1 s | 4 × 4, rest 60 s | 16 | matches bone Rung 5 |
| 4 | **Broad jump, stick** — max horizontal jump, land soft and hold 1 s | 4 × 3, rest 75 s | 12 | the §7 benchmark movement |
| 5 | **Repeated countermovement jumps** — 3 max jumps in a row, minimal ground time, soft knees | 4 × 3, rest 90 s | 12 | the first true rebound rung |
| 6 | **Drop jump, 12"** — step off a 12" box, land and immediately jump as high as possible, stick the second landing | 3 × 4, rest 90 s | 12 (+12 landings) | top of the ladder; 18" is never used |

- **Rungs 1–2 advance after 1 clean bout; rungs 3–5 after 3 clean bouts** (not necessarily
  consecutive). Rung 6 unlocks after **6 clean bouts at rung 5** and no knee, Achilles or
  foot Pain Response Protocol in the last 8 weeks.
- **Ceiling.** Rung 6 is the top. Beyond it progress by direction (lateral and diagonal
  broad jumps, single-leg stick at rung 3 or 4 doses), never by height or contact count.
- **Single-leg variants** are allowed from rung 3 at the same dose per leg once the user
  has 3 clean bouts at the two-leg rung; they are logged at the same rung with "SL".
- **Contacts per bout stay under 60** (rungs 1–3) or **under 40** (rungs 4–6). A standalone
  bout may combine one lower rung with the upper rung, never two lower rungs.

### Upper ladder (throw and push)

With a medicine or slam ball (6–10 lb to start) and a wall or open floor:

| Rung | Movement | Dose per bout |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tall-kneeling chest pass** to a wall, catch the rebound or let it drop | 3 × 5, rest 60 s |
| 2 | **Standing chest pass**, step into it | 3 × 5, rest 60 s |
| 3 | **Overhead slam** — ball overhead, slam to the floor, hinge not round | 3 × 5, rest 60 s |
| 4 | **Rotational scoop throw** to a wall, each side | 3 × 4/side, rest 75 s |
| 5 | **Rotational throw with a step**, each side; ball weight +2–4 lb at rungs 2–5 once a rung is clean twice | 3 × 4/side, rest 90 s |

Without a ball, the upper ladder **is** the upper-limb ladder of `bone-loading.md` §4 (wall
fall-catch through clap push-up), run under this file's dose and gates; the two files log
the same bout. A user with both modules active never does two upper ladders.

### Standalone bouts ("power bout", "jumps today", "plyos")

A bout is the lower rung at full dose plus the upper rung at full dose, after a 3-min
general warm-up (`warmup-and-mobility.md` §1, `LEGS` joint prep) and the leg prehab block
if one exists. About 12–15 min. Prescribed when the opener asks for one, or when a
`power` reminder fired. Placement:

1. **48 h between lower-ladder bouts**, counting primers: two leg-loading days in a row is
   fine, two jump bouts in a row is not.
2. A standalone lower bout runs **at least 24 h from a leg-loading session** on either side
   where the schedule offers such a day; where it does not, the bout becomes that session's
   primer instead of a standalone. Upper rungs may run any day.
3. Never on the same day as a HIIT session, and never within 48 h after one that used
   running or stairs.
4. Not a rotation day, never a miss, never the HIIT slot, and **vigorous**: a standalone
   bout counts toward the 3-in-7-days cardio cap of `conditioning-hiit.md` §2 as one
   session.

### Gates (checked before prescribing)

- **Pain.** Any joint sensation during a rep ends the bout and drops one rung. Pain → the
  Pain Response Protocol in `injury-protocols.md`.
- **Knee, Achilles, foot or hip protocol open** (any state but CLOSED): the lower ladder is
  **paused**, nothing is prescribed from it, rungs are frozen. It resumes at one rung down
  when the protocol closes, and the first bout back is 2 × 3.
- **Shoulder or elbow protocol open**: the upper ladder is paused the same way. **Back
  protocol open**: no slams or rotational throws (rungs 3–5 of the upper ladder); chest
  passes may continue.
- **Readiness flag** (`readiness.md`): no power work of any kind today, primer included.
  One line in Session Notes. The flag changes nothing in state.
- **Layoff**: more than 14 days since the last bout → drop one rung on each ladder.
- **Bone Doubles and the primer on the same day** are fine: the primer precedes the ramp,
  Bone Doubles follow the maintenance set, and the §2 time-limit order decides what is cut.

## 5. Blocks, deloads and flags

| Situation | Primer | Standalone bouts |
|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy block | 3 × 3–5 | as placed in §4 |
| Strength block | 3 × 3–5, plus the §3 cue on every main lift | as placed in §4 |
| Deload week | **2 × 3**, same rung, every session | none: the week's power work is the primer |
| `SLEEP`, `FATIGUE`, `SORE` or `ILL` flag | none today | none today |
| Deload-parameter session under `ILL` | none | none |
| Pain Response Protocol opening today | none | none; ladder gates in §4 apply from the next session |

A power bout or primer that was not performed is **not a miss**: the tally reports what
happened and nothing else moves.

## 6. Rhythm: the weekly tally

A rolling 7-day tally in `training-state.md`, printed in the coaching view whenever a POWER
section prints:

```
Power week (last 7 days): lower 1/2 · upper 1/2 · last bout 2026-10-24 (L3 / U2)
```

| Item | Minimum (maintain) | Target (build) |
|---|---|---|
| Lower-ladder bouts (primer or standalone) | 1 / week | 2 / week |
| Upper-ladder bouts | 1 / week | 2 / week |

- **Nudge rule.** Below the minimum on either line → one line in Session Notes ("Power:
  no lower bout in the last 7 days — minimum is 1"). Once per session, never repeated.
- **Reminders.** `kind: "power"` in `reminders.md` §2, opt-in like every reminder; the
  message carries today's rungs and doses and is edited in the same commit as any rung
  change (`reminders.md` §1 principle 4).

## 7. Benchmark (every 8 weeks)

Two numbers, taken fresh, on a standalone bout day or before a lower primer, never after
lifting:

1. **Standing broad jump**: 3 attempts, best distance in inches from the toe line to the
   nearest heel, landing stuck. Needs a tape measure and nothing else. Available from rung 3
   onward (the user must be allowed to land a jump); before that the benchmark is deferred,
   not substituted.
2. **Seated chest throw** (ball users only): seated against a wall, both hands, best of 3,
   distance to first bounce, same ball every time.

Record date, result and rung in state. A benchmark that falls more than 10% from the last
one with no layoff or flag in between is a Weekly Snapshot line and a question, not a
rung change. The `state:benchmark` reminder kind covers this benchmark as it does HIIT and
kettlebell. While the scorecard in `benchmarks.md` is active, its due date and table own
these two tests; the protocol here is unchanged.

## 8. Output, reporting and state

**Coaching view.** When the primer runs, a section between Prehab and Warmup Ramp:

```
### Power primer (3 min)
- Power week (last 7 days): lower [n]/2 · upper [n]/2
- [Rung movement] (L[rung]): 3 x [reps], rest 60 s — every rep max intent, reset between reps
  > [one line: rung status / clean-bout count / gate]
```

A standalone bout uses the quick-session header style (`quick-sessions.md` §6):
`## [Day] — Power bout (15 min)`, then General Warm-up, Prehab (legs block, if any), and a
`### Bout` section with one line per rung. "Not a rotation day" on the Session Notes line.

**Checklist.** A `POWER` section after `PREHAB` and before `WARMUP`, one line per set,
rest once per movement, no load field on bodyweight jumps, the ball weight as the load field
on throws:

```
POWER
Box jump 12" (L2) — R1m
Box jump 12" (L2) — Set 1 — 4
Box jump 12" (L2) — Set 2 — 4
Box jump 12" (L2) — Set 3 — 4

Standing chest pass (U2) — R1m
Standing chest pass (U2) — Set 1 — 8 lb — 5
```

**Reporting.** Unchanged lines = performed at the prescribed dose, clean. A deleted or `x`
line = not performed, not a miss. A shortened set ("3 then height dropped") logs the count
and still counts as a clean bout. A reported distance or height is logged as data. Any
sensation word routes to the Pain Response Protocol and ends the bout at that rep.

**State** (`training-state.md`, `## Power Module (OPTIONAL)`; until the user opts in:
`Rules: system/power-and-plyometrics.md. Not started.`):

```
## Power Module (OPTIONAL)
Rules: `system/power-and-plyometrics.md`. Started 2026-10-26.
Lower ladder: rung 3 (countermovement jump, stick) · clean bouts at rung: 1/3 · paused: no
Upper ladder: rung 2 (standing chest pass, 8 lb) · clean bouts at rung: 2/2 (advance next) · ball: 8 lb
Rolling 7 days: lower 1/2 · upper 1/2 · last bout: 2026-10-24 (L3 / U2, primer)
Benchmark: broad jump 78" (2026-10-20) · chest throw 14 ft (2026-10-20) · next due 2026-12-15
Bout log: 2026-10-24 L3 clean (primer, FB-A) · 2026-10-22 L3 clean · 2026-10-20 L2 clean · …
```

**Weekly Snapshot** adds one line: bouts vs target per ladder, rung changes, and the
benchmark when due.

## 9. What this module never does

- Never overrides an injury exclusion, a Pain Response Protocol, a readiness flag or a time
  limit.
- Never changes the rotation, block, rep windows, RIR ramp, rest or any lift's progression.
- Never counts a jump or throw toward §11 weekly volume.
- Never prescribes a depth drop from above 12", a loaded jump, or a jump to failure.
- Never logs a bout that was not reported.

**Bone module interaction.** When both modules are active, one jump bout a day serves
both: a lower-ladder bout at rung 1 logs as bone impact Rung 2, rung 3 as bone Rung 5, and
rung 2, 4, 5 or 6 as bone Rung 5 (bone's ladder tops out at drop landings, which rung 6
includes). The bone tally and the power tally each record it. The bone module's knee gate
for its Rungs 5–6 and this file's knee gate are the same gate; whichever file is stricter
on the day wins. Bone bouts at Rung 1 (heel drops) and Rung 4 (multidirectional single-leg
hops) are bone-only and do not feed the power tally.

**Session-end review hooks** (per `operating-protocol.md` §6): flag for review if the user
skips the POWER section for 3 consecutive weeks (ask whether to keep offering it or shrink
it to the minimum), or a rung produces a sensation twice (ask whether that rung stays).
