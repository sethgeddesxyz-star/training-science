# Aerobic Training Heart Rate (added 2026-10-16, user request)

This card sets the **ceiling** for easy aerobic work. How much, when, and where it is logged
is `aerobic-base.md` (added 2026-10-25).

## MAF 180 Formula (Phil Maffetone)

**Maximum aerobic heart rate = 180 − age**

Train at or slightly below this number. A common working range is the MAF number minus 10, up to the MAF number.

### Adjustments

| Situation | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Recovering from major illness or surgery, or on regular medication | −10 |
| Injured, training stalled or regressing, frequent colds or flu, allergies or asthma | −5 |
| Training consistently for up to 2 years without the issues above | 0 |
| Training consistently for 2+ years, still improving, no injuries | +5 |

**Example:** A healthy, consistent 40-year-old has a ceiling of 180 − 40 = **140 bpm** (working range 130–140 bpm).

### Caveat

The formula comes from Maffetone's coaching experience, not controlled trials. Its main value is practical: it keeps easy sessions truly easy.

## Zone 2 Training

Zone 2 is the research-oriented version of the same idea. Iñigo San Millán and others have popularized it.

- **Lab definition:** roughly the highest effort at which blood lactate stays near 2 mmol/L.
- **Field approximations** (no lab testing):
  - About 60–70% of max heart rate. Estimate max HR as **208 − 0.7 × age** (Tanaka formula).
  - **Talk test:** you can speak in full sentences but would rather not sing.

## How they compare

For most people, the MAF number and the Zone 2 ceiling land close to each other.
