# Benchmarks — one scorecard every 8 weeks (added 2026-10-26, user request)

Four files each ran their own benchmark on their own clock: HIIT (`conditioning-hiit.md`
§7), kettlebell (`kettlebell.md` §5), power (`power-and-plyometrics.md` §7) and balance
(`balance.md` §7). Nothing joined them, nothing measured the whole person, and a user with
none of those modules was never tested at all. This file is the **one schedule and the one
results table**: a short set of equipment-free tests everyone takes, the module benchmarks
folded in when their module is active, every 8 weeks, in the deload week, with a trend and
a norm band where a good one exists. It is the part of the program a user can show someone
in two minutes.

**Scope.** The scorecard reports and trends. It never changes a load, a rung, a block or a
target: every rule that progresses something lives in that thing's file. A band is a line,
not a verdict, and never advice.

Read this file when a scorecard is **due** (§2, the `state:benchmark` reminder or the
Weekly Snapshot says so), when the opener says "test", "benchmark", "scorecard", "how am I
doing" or "retest", and once at intake to set the baseline and choose the aerobic and grip
options. Otherwise it is not read. If the user declines the scorecard at intake, the state
says "Not started", this file is not read, and each module's own benchmark runs under its
own file exactly as before.

---

## 1. What the science says (short version; references in §9)

- **Simple field tests predict hard outcomes.** Grip strength predicted all-cause
  mortality better than systolic blood pressure across 17 countries, about 16% higher risk
  per 5 kg lower (Leong 2015, PURE). Muscular strength in general carries the same
  relationship (García-Hermoso 2018 meta-analysis). The ability to stand on one leg for
  10 s (Araujo 2022), to sit down to and rise from the floor without hands (Brito 2014),
  and push-up capacity (Yang 2019: 40+ push-ups vs fewer than 10, 96% lower cardiovascular
  events over 10 years in active men) all carry independent prognostic weight.
- **Cardiorespiratory fitness is the strongest of them.** Each 1-MET rise in measured
  fitness cuts all-cause mortality by about 13% (Kodama 2009), with no upper limit found
  in 122,007 patients (Mandsager 2018). Field estimates (Cooper 1968; Kline 1987) are
  coarse but repeatable, and the trend is what the scorecard tracks.
- **Function tests have age norms.** The 30-s chair stand (Jones 1999; Rikli & Jones
  1999), one-leg stance (Springer 2007), Timed Up and Go (Shumway-Cook 2000) and grip
  (Dodds 2014; EWGSOP2 cut-points, Cruz-Jentoft 2019) each have published bands, so a
  result can be placed, once, without interpretation.
- **Retests are only as good as their conditions.** Learning effects and day-to-day noise
  swamp small changes; a meaningful change is one larger than the test's own error, which
  needs the same test, order, warm-up, time of day and rest state every time (Hopkins
  2000). Hence the fixed protocol in §3 and the fixed order in §4.
- **Periodic testing is standard practice** (ACSM 2021) and, for a self-coached program,
  the only feedback that is not a rep count.

## 2. Schedule

1. **Baseline at intake** (or at the first session after opting in), spread over the
   first two sessions so no single day is a test day. A lift's e1RM is not tested; it is
   read from state once the lift has a qualifying set.
2. **Every 8 weeks** after the baseline, taken **in the first deload week at or after the
   due date** (blocks are 4 weeks plus a deload, so in practice every second deload).
   Deload sessions are light enough to test around. If the next deload is more than 2
   weeks past the due date, the strength and balance set runs on a rest day instead and
   the aerobic test takes that week's HIIT or aerobic slot.
3. **Never in Strength Week 4** (the AMRAP week), never within 48 h after a HIIT session,
   and **never on a readiness flag** (`readiness.md`): a flagged day defers the whole
   scorecard, not just one test.
4. **A test on a joint with an open Pain Response Protocol is deferred, not substituted**,
   and marked "deferred (PRP)" in the table. The rest of the card runs.
5. The `state:benchmark` reminder kind (`reminders.md` §2) carries the due date; the
   Weekly Snapshot says "scorecard due" from the due date until it is taken. Overdue is
   not a miss.

## 3. The tests

### Core set (everyone; no equipment beyond a wall, a chair, a tape measure and a timer)

| # | Test | Protocol | Result | Band source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One-leg stance**, eyes open | shoes off, arms free, hands not touching, best of 3 per side, stopped at 60 s | seconds, R and L | Springer 2007 by age; under 10 s: Araujo 2022 |
| 2 | **30-s chair stand** | standard chair, arms crossed, full stand and full sit, count in 30 s | reps | Jones 1999 / CDC STEADI by age and sex |
| 3 | **Push-up max** | continuous, strict, at the user's current ladder step (`bodyweight-ladders.md` Ladder 1); stop at the first failed or ugly rep | reps at step | Yang 2019 (full push-up only); a step change breaks the trend and is noted |
| 4 | **Grip** | *with a dynamometer:* standing, elbow straight, best of 3 per hand, 15 s between · *without:* **dead hang** max time, overhand, bar or beam, one attempt | kg per hand, or seconds | Dodds 2014 centiles and EWGSOP2 cut-points (27 kg men / 16 kg women); dead hang has no validated band and trends only. The Isometrics Module reads 30% of the dynamometer number for handgrip bouts (`isometrics.md` §2) |
| 5 | **Sit and rise** (optional, floor needed) | from standing, sit on the floor and stand back up; start at 10, lose 1 point per hand, knee, forearm or side of leg used, 0.5 per visible wobble | score 0–10 | Brito 2014 (under 8 carries higher risk) |
| 6 | **Aerobic** (one option, chosen at intake, same every time) | **(a)** 12-min run/walk for distance (Cooper 1968) · **(b)** 1-mile brisk walk, time and HR at the end (Kline 1987) · **(c)** bike 20-min steady test at a fixed wattage, average HR over the last 10 min, plus a 5-min max for average watts (`conditioning-hiit.md` §7) · **(d)** a wearable's VO2max estimate, trend only | distance, time, HR, watts, or ml/kg/min | Cooper: VO2max ≈ (metres − 504.9) / 44.73; Kline for the walk; both to METs (÷ 3.5) against Kodama 2009: under 8 METs low, 8–10 moderate, over 10 high |
| 7 | **Standing broad jump** (only when cleared) | best of 3, toe line to nearest heel, landing stuck; **only** with no knee, ankle or foot protocol open and either the Power Module at rung 3+ or the user already jumps | inches | none; trend and Markovic 2004 reliability only |
| 8 | **Main-lift e1RM** (read, not tested) | the stored e1RM of the squat, hinge, h_push and h_pull main lifts on the day of the card | lb, and change since last card | none; relative to bodyweight only if the user volunteers a weight (bodyweight is not tracked, by decision) |

### Module set (folded in while the module is active; each test keeps its own rules)

| Module | Tests | Owner of the protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Balance | one-leg stance (core #1 counts), **four-stage balance test**, **Timed Up and Go** (optional) | `balance.md` §7 |
| Power | broad jump (core #7 counts), **seated chest throw** (ball users) | `power-and-plyometrics.md` §7 |
| HIIT | the bike steady test and 5-min max (core #6 option c counts); on another aerobic option the HIIT benchmark is this card's aerobic test, not a second one | `conditioning-hiit.md` §7 |
| Kettlebell | the §5 standards attempted that cycle (at most two, one attempt each) | `kettlebell.md` §5 |

While the scorecard is active, those files' "every 8 weeks" **is this schedule**: one due
date, one table, no module runs its own clock. A module benchmark is never taken twice in a
cycle because two files asked for it.

## 4. The test session

**Strength and balance set** (core 1–5, 7, and the balance and power module tests), one
session, about 25–30 min, in this order so the fatiguing tests come last and the fragile
ones first:

1. General warm-up (`warmup-and-mobility.md` §1, `LEGS` joint prep), 3 min.
2. One-leg stance → four-stage → Timed Up and Go (balance first, fresh).
3. Broad jump → seated chest throw (power second, still fresh; a short rest after).
4. Grip (or dead hang).
5. Push-up max.
6. 30-s chair stand → sit and rise.

**Aerobic test** on a separate day, at least 24 h from the set above and from any leg
session, replacing that week's HIIT or aerobic slot; it counts as one session toward the
cardio cap. Same route, bike, time of day and warm-up every time.

**Kettlebell standards** on the deload week's kettlebell session, as `kettlebell.md` §5
already says.

A test cut short, refused, or stopped by a sensation is logged "not taken" with the reason;
a sensation routes to the Pain Response Protocol as anywhere else. One attempt beyond the
stated best-of is never offered.

## 5. Bands and trend

- **Trend first.** Every row shows the result, the change since the last card and the
  change since baseline. The trend is the scorecard's product.
- **A band prints once per row**, only when the test has a cited norm **and** the inputs
  the norm needs (age always; sex where the norm is split) are on record. The user's sex is
  asked only at the first card and only for this purpose; "prefer not" means the trend
  alone. No band is ever restated in later sessions or turned into a recommendation.
- **Flag rule.** A strength-type result (2, 3, 4, 7, 8, aerobic) more than 10% below the
  last card, or a balance result more than 25% below, with no layoff, readiness flag or
  protocol in between, is a Weekly Snapshot line and one question ("retest in 2 weeks, or
  leave it?"). The engine changes nothing on its own.
- **The one-leg stance under 10 s, the sit-and-rise under 8, and the Timed Up and Go over
  12 s** each get the single cited line their owning file already specifies, once per card.
  Nothing else is said.

## 6. Output, reporting and state

**Coaching view.** The test session uses the quick-session header style
(`quick-sessions.md` §6): `## [Day] — Scorecard (30 min)`, the general warm-up, then one
`### Tests` section listing the tests in §4 order with their protocol in one line each, and
a Session Notes line "Not a rotation day; the deload session is still due." The aerobic test
prints as the HIIT §9 variant or the aerobic-base three-line session, labelled "(benchmark)".

**Checklist.** One `BENCHMARK` block, one line per result field, the user fills the last
field:

```
BENCHMARK
One-leg stance — R — [s]
One-leg stance — L — [s]

Four-stage balance — highest stage held — [1-4]

Standing broad jump — best of 3 — [in]

Dead hang — max — [s]

Push-up (step 6) — max — [reps]

30-s chair stand — [reps]

Sit and rise — score — [0-10]
```

**Reporting.** A number in the last field is the result. An empty field or `x` is "not
taken", not a miss. "Touched" on a stance line logs the seconds reached. Free text with a
sensation word ends that test's line and routes to the Pain Response Protocol.

`Rules: system/benchmarks.md. Not started.`):


**Weekly Snapshot** adds one line: "scorecard due [date]" from the due date until taken,
then, once, the card's headline (tests up / flat / down) with any §5 flag.

## 7. What this file never does

- Never changes a load, rung, window, block, target or reminder: it reads and records.
- Never turns a band into advice, repeats a band, or asks for bodyweight.
- Never tests a joint under an open protocol, a flagged day, or Strength Week 4.
- Never runs a module benchmark twice in a cycle, and never runs one at all for a module
  that is not active.
- Never logs a result that was not reported.

**Session-end review hooks** (per `operating-protocol.md` §6): flag for review if two
consecutive cards are declined or deferred in full (ask whether to keep the scorecard, or
shrink it to the one-leg stance and the aerobic test), or a test produces a sensation twice
(ask whether it stays on the card).

---

## 8. Intake additions

At intake, or when opting in later, three questions, batched: which aerobic option (a–d,
by what the user has; the walk is the default for anyone without a bike or a running
habit); whether a dynamometer or a bar exists (grip or dead hang); and whether to record sex
for the norm bands (optional). Then the baseline is scheduled across the first two sessions.
